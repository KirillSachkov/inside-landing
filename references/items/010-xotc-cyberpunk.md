## Visual analysis

- **Palette:** near-black ground (~#0A0E0D); a single neon lime accent (~#C0F236 / #B8E82E) reserved exclusively for CTAs, small highlights, and the "ACCEPTED" pill in the trade UI; illustration tones are desaturated slate blue-greens (~#3E5560, #5B7585, #7E97A0) with lime rim-lighting; headline white is a slightly green-tinted off-white (~#F0F4E6); body text is mid-grey (~#9BA3A0). Effectively a 1-accent discipline: lime = action.
- **Typography:** one techno display family for all headings — extra-bold, expanded, squared letterforms with slightly rounded corners (Orbitron/Mokoto territory), all-caps, tight tracking, set at enormous scale (headline spans most of the frame width). Section titles ("WHAT IS XOTC?") are the same face, obliqued/slanted and left-aligned stacked on two lines. Body and UI labels are a small neutral grotesque/monospace-flavoured sans in grey — a strict two-tier system: shout or whisper, nothing between.
- **Composition & grid:** everything hangs on a central vertical axis; the hero text is centered over the illustration's vanishing point (the figure's sightline points into the headline). Sections are full-width but content stays in a centered ~12-col container; the diagram card is roughly 2/3 page width, centered. Low element count per screen — headline + one line + one button in the hero.
- **Graphics:** full-bleed 2D illustration with flat cel shading and lime glow edges; HUD frame with corner brackets around the hero (a "viewport into the world" device); halftone dot-cluster textures at frame corners and on the diagram card; crosshair/target glyphs as punctuation; a 3D-rendered tram breaking the card border (the one element that escapes the grid — classic "object exits the frame" technique); the trade-UI mini-window ("PEPE 20,000,000 / VALUE: $32,590 → ACCEPT TRADE … ACCEPTED") rendered as a real product screenshot styled into the scene.
- **Motion:** static screenshot — nothing visible. Reasonably inferable: the HUD brackets, glowing sign ("XOTC" on the tower), and trade window are built for parallax/glitch micro-animation, and the tram likely scrolls with the card; not verifiable from the image.

## Signature techniques

- **HUD framing:** thin border + corner brackets turn the hero illustration into a monitored viewport — instant genre signal.
- **One-color discipline:** lime appears only on interactive/affirmative elements (CTA, ACCEPTED pill, button in nav); the entire illustration borrows the same lime as rim light, so UI and artwork feel like one world.
- **Breakout object:** the 3D tram overlapping the card border destroys the flatness of an otherwise rigid grid.
- **Diagram-as-evidence:** the swap mechanic is explained by a minimal "PERSON 01 ⇄ PERSON 02" glyph plus a real-looking trade UI window instead of paragraphs.
- **Halftone dot clusters** as cheap texture at corners — adds density without noise.
- **Slanted stacked section titles** — same font, italicized, instantly separates section headers from the hero headline.

## Takeaways for Inside

- **The one principle to take:** the single-accent discipline on a dark ground — one signal color meaning "action/affirmation", everything else desaturated. Fits our audience (engineers read UI states, not decoration) and our character (technological without hype): we could keep the strict palette logic while swapping cyberpunk neon for a calmer engineering accent. Hypothesis, not decision.
- Secondary takeaways: explain a mechanic with a compact diagram + a real artifact window (maps well to our ADRs/diagrams/code); section titles slanted/stacked as a cheap way to make dark pages feel authored.
- **What must not be copied:** the cyberpunk genre itself — neon city illustration, HUD brackets, glitch/techno display font, 3D tram. All of it reads "crypto/game hype", the opposite of "AI as practice, not decoration" and "honest about boundaries". Also the shouting display type at hero scale would fight our density of real content.
- **License constraints:** the display typeface is genre-standard (Orbitron is OFL; Mokoto is paid) — any similar face must be license-checked before use. The city illustration and 3D tram are bespoke commissioned artwork — not reusable; the techniques are, the assets are not. Copy lines ("DEFI OTC TRADING FOR EVERYONE") obviously not reusable.
