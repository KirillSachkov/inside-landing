## What this is

Hero section of **ainrum**, an AI data-security SaaS ("AI That Keeps Data Secured.").

## Visual analysis

- **Palette:** warm light scheme. Cream/off-white background (≈ #F4EFE6, subtle
  vertical gradient slightly darker at the top); illustration lines and secondary
  text in warm taupe/brown-gray (≈ #8A7B6A); headline and logo in near-black warm
  brown (≈ #2B2018); primary CTA fill dark coffee brown (≈ #3D2E20) with white
  text; ghost CTA fill pale beige (≈ #E9E2D5) with dark text. One saturated accent
  color only: orange (≈ #E8622A) used sparingly — gauge needles and switches on
  the control panel, chip tops on the conveyor cubes.
- **Typography:** one geometric grotesque family throughout, rounded-leaning
  (Inter/Poppins/General-Sans territory; rounded "a", circular counters). Headline
  ≈ 44–48 px at this width, medium weight, tight leading (~1.05), slightly
  negative tracking, ends with a period. Subline ≈ 13–14 px, regular, muted taupe,
  generous line-height. Nav and CTAs ≈ 12–13 px, medium. No serif, no mono — the
  "technical" feeling comes from the drawing, not the type.
- **Composition & grid:** no visible column grid; the composition is built on the
  isometric axes (~30°). The conveyor belt enters from the top-left corner and
  exits bottom-right, creating a diagonal that passes behind the copy block and
  leads the eye across the whole hero. Copy sits in the clear zone the conveyor
  leaves open. Very low element count: nav + 4 text elements + 1 illustration.
- **Graphics:** large thin-line isometric technical illustration — a machining
  hub (square machine with rounded corners, recessed top cavity, visible bolts at
  the corners, a dark slot/screen on the front face) fed by a conveyor of rounded
  cubes; some cubes carry small orange chips. Blueprint conventions: dashed guide
  lines under the belt and parts, even 1–1.5 px stroke weights, no fills except
  soft beige shading. Small black pictograms (straight arrow, diagonal arrow,
  circular arrow + sparkle) sit at the illustration's lower right, reading as
  interaction/processing hints. A detached isometric control panel with two orange
  gauges and toggle switches floats beside the machine.
- **Motion:** static screenshot — none visible. Reasonably inferable: the conveyor
  + arrow pictograms strongly suggest a looping belt animation (cubes entering the
  hub, processed cubes leaving with orange chips); unconfirmed.

## Signature techniques

- **Isometric blueprint as the hero** — the entire product metaphor (raw data in →
  secured data out) is told by one technical drawing; no screenshots, no photos.
- **One-accent discipline** — a fully desaturated warm palette with a single
  orange accent that marks exactly the "value" spots (gauges, chips, switches).
- **Diagonal conveyor composition** — the belt's path doubles as the layout's
  leading line, connecting top-left to bottom-right and framing the copy block.
- **Warm near-black instead of pure black** — headline, logo and primary CTA use
  warm browns, keeping a technical subject from feeling cold or corporate.
- **Centered-logo nav** — logo in the middle, links split left/right; calm,
  editorial balance instead of the usual logo-left SaaS pattern.

## Takeaways for Inside

- **The one principle to take:** let one precise technical illustration carry the
  product metaphor while everything else stays quiet. It fits our audience
  directly — practicing developers read engineering drawings as a signal of real
  work, and an isometric "hub" of our pipeline (Telegram input → processing →
  artifacts: code, diagrams, ADRs) could communicate the product's structure
  better than any stock visual. The one-accent discipline also matches "dense in
  value but not overloaded". Hypothesis, not decision.
- **What must not be copied:** the SaaS polish around it — the generic
  benefit-headline ("AI That Keeps Data Secured.") with a period, the "Started
  now"/"Get Started" double CTA marketing voice, and the rounded-friendly
  grotesque with soft beige shading; Inside should read more authorial and less
  conversion-templated. The "AI" framing as decoration is exactly the hype tone
  we avoid — for us AI is practice and would need to show in the drawing's
  content, not in a buzzword headline.
- **License constraints:** illustration and wordmark are brand-owned artwork —
  technique (isometric thin-line, dashed blueprint guides, single accent) is
  borrowable, the drawing itself is not. If the typeface is a commercial rounded
  grotesque (e.g. Poppins is OFL; General Sans is free; Inter is OFL — safe
  lookalikes exist), licensing is a non-issue. Screenshot used for internal
  analysis only.
