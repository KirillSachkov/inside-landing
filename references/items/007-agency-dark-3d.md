## Signature techniques

- **Spotlight ellipse:** a warm flat oval under the hero illustration that reads as a pool of light — instantly warms a dark page without gradients or glow effects.
- **One highlighted sibling:** in a row of identical dark cards, exactly one is filled with the accent color and slightly enlarged/top-shifted — a cheap, powerful "selected/featured" state.
- **Hand-drawn underline as the sole human touch:** one marker squiggle under one word breaks the corporate geometry and signals an author, not a corporation.
- **Single-family typography:** headline, nav, cards all in one grotesque; hierarchy done by size/weight/color only.
- **Isometric tech object as hero art:** a mechanical keyboard (an audience-native object for developers) rendered in 3D instead of abstract shapes or stock photos.

## Takeaways for Inside

- **The one principle to take:** *warm spotlight + one highlighted card.* A dark engineering landing can feel alive and authored with a single warm-lit illustration zone and exactly one accent-colored card among dark siblings (e.g. the recommended subscription tier, or the current build-series entry among past ones). This fits our audience because it's specificity through restraint — developers read "one thing is selected here" as a deliberate recommendation, not marketing noise. Hypothesis, not decision.
- **What must not be copied:** the recruiting context itself (job-card semantics, "Send a resume" CTA) is off-mission; the playful floating confetti risks drifting into generic startup playfulness; isometric 3D renders are expensive-looking but decorative — Inside's graphics should carry evidence (code, diagrams, artifacts), not just mood. The agency-generic headline pattern ("X is looking for Y") must not be reused.
- **License constraints:** the grotesque looks like a commercial geometric sans (Graphik/Neue Haas/Gilroy class) — do not assume a free equivalent; Inter, Manrope or Onest cover the same register under OFL. The isometric 3D illustration is custom agency artwork — cannot be reused or closely imitated asset-wise; any keyboard/desk 3D scene for us must be commissioned or generated. Copy is trivial but brand-specific; don't reuse phrasing.
