## What this is

Two pixel-art scenes of retro hardware overgrown with plants: (1) a white CRT
and a purple GameCube-like console with a controller in grass on near-black;
(2) an isometric full retro PC workplace — CRT showing a pixel "S." logo next
to a node-graph glyph, tower, keyboard, mouse, coffee mug, plants — on a grassy
floating-island base, dark plum background.

## Structure

Single-subject scenes, not layouts. The second image's screen content
("S." + graph glyph) is a direct hint at a brand mark on the device screen.

## Visual analysis

- **Palette:** (1) near-black `#14201C`-ish bg, saturated grass greens, purple
  console; (2) dark plum `#241B25`-ish bg, slate-blue hardware, moss greens,
  violet screen glow (`#7B5CF0`-ish) with light pixel "S.".
- **Typography:** pixel lettering ("S." on screen) — display use only.
- **Composition & grid:** isometric/three-quarter, floating-island pedestal,
  subject centered with air around it.
- **Graphics:** true pixel art — limited palette per object, visible clusters,
  dithering on grass; plants soften the tech, "nature reclaims hardware" mood.
- **Motion:** static; pixel scenes animate cheaply via sprite frames
  (screen flicker, blinking cursor, leaf sway) with `steps()` timing.

## Signature techniques

- Screen as the brand carrier: the device is generic-retro, the glowing "S."
  makes it owned.
- Floating-island pedestal: the object gets a "world" without a full
  background scene.

## Takeaways for Inside

- **The one principle to take (hypothesis):** pixel-art hero object would
  connect the landing to the existing pixel-art Telegram avatars — brand
  continuity no other direction uses. A pixel device with a live terminal
  screen ("S_" cursor blink) is cheap to animate frame-by-frame.
- **What must not be copied:** the overgrown-garden fantasy mood (reads
  cozy-gaming, off "professional" character) — if pixel, keep the device on a
  plain pedestal without foliage; dark gaming-plum palette.
- **License constraints:** artist-signed pixel work ("PIXEL JESS") — do not
  reuse or closely imitate; pixel style itself is free to use.
