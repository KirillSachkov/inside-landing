## What this is

Mobile-width capture of the GDevelop (no-code 2D game engine) landing page, showing
three sections in a row: the pixel-art hero, a white "how it works" section, and a
dark "publish" section. Note: the rounded-corner overlay buttons at the top-left
(back arrow) and bottom-right (expand, rotate) are artifacts of the capture tool,
not part of the site. The site itself has a minimal top nav: pixel-ish "G" logo,
text links (Features, Games, Learn, Blog), and a white "Download" pill top-right.

## Structure

1. **Hero (dark, full-width illustration):** centered headline "Game making made
   easy", one short two-line subcopy, two pill CTAs side by side — cyan "Try it
   online" (gradient, glowing) and white "Download". The pixel-art scene (floating
   islands, moon, parrot, glowing portal, coins) wraps around the text block rather
   than sitting beside it; copy is laid on the sky's negative space.
2. **White section:** centered heading "Game creation, intuitive for all" (second
   line, with "intuitive" in purple), one centered paragraph, then two small
   rounded "event card" mockups showing engine logic ("Player is in collision with
   coin…"). Flanked left and right by tall pixel-art column illustrations (cartridge
   column, chest + ladder column).
3. **Dark section:** centered heading "Publish your game in two clicks" ("two
   clicks" in cyan), one centered paragraph listing targets (web, iOS, Android,
   Steam, Facebook Gaming, Itch.io, Newgrounds, Microsoft Store…), then a row of
   small platform-icon chips in dark rounded squares.

Rhythm: dark → white → dark, with a wavy cloud-shaped seam between the hero and
the white section — the hero's clouds spill into the next section and become the
divider.

## Visual analysis

- **Palette:** hero sky is a deep navy-to-purple gradient (approx. `#0B1035` at the
  top darkening the edges, shifting to violet `#3A2A6E`-range toward the horizon);
  accent cyan `#4DE0E5`-range (moon, portal glow, "Try it online" CTA, "two
  clicks"); purple/indigo `#4B55C8`-range for the "intuitive" highlight; pure white
  `#FFFFFF` sections with black body text; pixel art in flat mint-green
  (`#6FCF97`-range) grass, brown rock, red parrot, yellow coins. Roughly 90/10
  dark/light per dark section, 50/50 page-wide.
- **Typography:** rounded geometric sans-serif throughout, consistent with
  GDevelop's brand font (appears to be Rubik). Hero headline white, extra-bold,
  two lines, very large relative to the mobile viewport (~40px equivalent);
  section headings bold, ~24px equivalent, with one keyword recolored per heading
  (purple "intuitive", cyan "two clicks"); body copy small (~11–13px equivalent),
  centered, gray-on-white or white-on-dark, max ~45 characters per line.
- **Composition & grid:** strict center-aligned single column; content column is
  narrow (~640px at desktop scale) with generous empty margins; sections are
  loosely separated by color blocks, not rules or cards. Very low copy density —
  one heading, one paragraph, one supporting element per section.
- **Graphics:** custom pixel-art illustration is the entire visual identity of the
  hero — a full game scene (parallax-ready floating islands, hanging vines, stone
  portal with cyan glow, collectible coins, clouds) rendered behind the UI copy.
  Section 2 repeats pixel art as symmetric flanking columns. Tiny inline "event
  card" mockups show the product's actual logic-editor UI in miniature, acting as
  soft product screenshots. Platform icons in dark rounded-square chips.
- **Motion:** static screenshot; the hero is clearly built for parallax (layered
  islands, foreground clouds) and the event cards look like a looping animation
  stack, but no motion is visible in the capture — inference only.

## Signature techniques

- **Illustration as environment, not decoration:** the hero artwork is a full scene
  with its own depth (foreground islands at the edges, copy floating in the sky's
  empty middle) — text is placed *inside* the world, not next to an image.
- **Keyword color-highlight in headings:** each heading recolors exactly one or two
  words in the accent color ("intuitive", "two clicks"), making the value
  proposition scannable.
- **Shaped section seam:** a wavy cloud silhouette divides dark→white instead of a
  straight edge, keeping the transition on-theme.
- **Alternating dark/white blocks** with identical centered layout, so rhythm comes
  from color alone.
- **Dual CTA with clear hierarchy:** primary action (try online, glowing cyan) vs
  secondary (download, plain white pill).

## Takeaways for Inside

- **The one principle to take:** *one accent word per heading*. Recoloring a single
  keyword in each section heading gives instant scanning of the value prop without
  any extra design furniture — fits an engineering audience that skims, and matches
  Inside's density-without-noise goal. Hypothesis, not decision.
- **What must not be copied:** the pixel-art/game-scene aesthetic, the playful
  parrot-and-portal mood, and the rounded "cute" type feel — wrong product
  character (Inside is engineering evidence, not play). Also avoid the very sparse
  copy density; Inside needs more substance per section.
- **License constraints:** all pixel art, the "G" logo, and layout are GDevelop
  brand assets — not reusable. The style of keyword-highlighting and alternating
  color blocks is generic; any pixel or scene art we might commission would need
  its own license. Font (Rubik-like rounded sans) is not right for Inside
  regardless.
